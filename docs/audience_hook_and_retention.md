# Audience Hook and Retention for Evidence-Driven Videos

The first seconds should create curiosity without sacrificing evidence quality. The goal is not clickbait; it is a fast promise that the video will show the source, explain the stakes, and make a useful point.

## Opening 3 seconds

- Start with a concrete tension: a surprising official number, a policy change, a mismatch between hype and evidence, or a visible source screenshot.
- Show the source frame early so viewers know this is evidence-led.
- Use one short question or contrast, not an unsupported conclusion.

## Retention structure

1. Hook: what changed or what people are missing.
2. Evidence: show the official page/PDF/public reference and highlight the exact line.
3. Interpretation: explain only what the evidence supports.
4. Consequence: why it matters to the target audience.
5. Caveat: state uncertainty, limitations, or missing data.
6. Review prompt: invite verification rather than blind belief.

## Visual rhythm

- Alternate wide source context and zoomed quote bbox.
- Use dynamic subtitles for dense terms, but keep source highlights readable.
- Use public-reference clips sparingly and only when they add necessary context.
- Prefer short contact-sheet checks to verify that each high-energy visual still maps to a valid cue.

## Guardrails

- No title or hook may claim more than the evidence supports.
- Do not hide caveats until after a dramatic claim.
- Avoid decontextualized screenshots; include capture timestamp and URL in manifest.
- Any privacy-sensitive visual must pass the mosaic/review gate before render approval.
